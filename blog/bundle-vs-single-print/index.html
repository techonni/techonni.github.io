Bundle vs single print

One file is a poster. Seventeen files are a language. The price tells you which you are buying.

A single Bauhaus print is €14.95. The bundle is €99. Seventeen singles would be about €254. The bundle exists so that if you already want the set, you are not punished for honesty.

That is the only maths that matters. I am not running a coupon. I am not hiding the single price to push the pack. Both stay on the same grid.

Buy one when the wall is specific

You already know the picture. The pink arch. The black cube of lines. The eye. You can see the frame. In that case pay for one JPEG, print it once, stop. A bundle in that moment is clutter on a disk.

One print also lets you test a lab. If the first sheet comes back too warm or too thin, you have spent fifteen euros to learn the shop. Then you order the rest from a place you trust.

Buy the set when the room is empty

A new flat. A long corridor. A studio that needs a row, not a souvenir. Then seventeen files for €99 is the calmer ticket. You print three this month and leave the others named and ready.

After you pay the bundle, one button builds a zip in the browser. You keep Bauhaus_Bundle.zip next to the singles. Same files as the product pages. No watermark. No smaller “preview pack.”

What I would do

If I were walking into the shop cold, I would buy one print I could hang this week. Live with it. If I kept opening the other thumbnails, I would come back for the bundle and treat the first purchase as a sample. I would not buy four singles “to be safe.” Four singles is already more than a third of the set and still not the language.

The shop will not retire the singles when you buy the pack. You are not missing a future discount. You are choosing a folder size.
