How I ship a print-ready JPEG at 50 × 70

You do not get a parcel. You get a file built to hang. This is the whole path from pay to glass.

A Techonni print is a JPEG. Not a canvas in the post. Not a mystery PDF. One image, large enough to print at fifty by seventy centimetres, sharp enough to sit behind glass without looking cheap.

I picked that size because it is a common European frame. You can walk into a shop, buy a 50 × 70 frame with glass, and the paper drops in. No custom joinery. No waiting for a crate. The product is the file plus the decision to print it once, well.

What “print-ready” means here

Print-ready means the file already lives at the size you will hang. The Bauhaus files on the shop are 5906 × 8268 pixels. That is enough for 50 × 70 cm at about 300 dots per inch. If a lab asks for 300 dpi, you do not upsample. You send the JPEG as it is.

The colour is sRGB. Most consumer labs and most home printers expect that. I do not ship CMYK. I do not ship a layered file you have to flatten. If a print shop wants a different profile, they convert it. Your job is to download, not to retouch.

There is no bleed, no crop marks, no secret margin. The image is the poster. If you want a white border, the lab can add one. I would rather you see the work edge to edge on screen and decide.

What happens after you pay

Stripe takes the card. You come back to the product page with a paid flag. One button appears: Download print. That button is the JPEG. On the bundle page the button builds a zip of all seventeen files in the browser. Nothing arrives by email until Stripe sends its own receipt.

Keep the file. Name it as it arrives. If you lose it, you still have the receipt and I can send the same JPEG again. There is no licence key and no account wall. The file is yours to print for your wall. It is not a stock licence to resell.

How I would print it

Use a lab that prints posters, not a supermarket photo kiosk. Ask for 50 × 70 cm on matte or slightly textured paper. Gloss fights the glass. A heavy paper (170 g and up) sits flatter in the frame.

Do not scale the file down “to save ink” and then blow it up at the counter. Send the original. If the lab preview looks soft, they used the wrong file. Send it again.

When the print comes back, let it flatten under a book overnight. Then the frame. Glass or acrylic, your call. The point of the product is that last quiet rectangle on the wall — not the checkout.

If you only want one, start with the print that already lives in your head. If you want the room to feel like a set, take the bundle and print three first. The rest can wait in the folder.
